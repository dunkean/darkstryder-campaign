The space between land­ ing spaces and the hangar wall is always bustling with cargo skiffs, flight crews, cus­ toms officers, hangar maintenance workers and binary load lifters all going about their business.

At the rear of the hangar, next to the passageway to the starport services prom­ enade, is the hangar customs and control office. Part of this office is dedicated to cus­ toms inspections. It contains customs of­ fices where records are kept, ready rooms ,for customs guards, barracks for the guards and quarters for customs officers, and a warehouse for contraband materials confis­ cated from smugglers.

The hangar control offices house the offic­ ers who control traffic and operations within the docking hangar. Here spacers can order repairs on their ship, supplies to be replen­ ished, and cargo to be loaded and unloaded by hangar crews. They may also request guards to be posted around the ship if they're carryingavaluable cargo. Ofcourse, all these services cost money.
