## STARWARS LORDSEXPANSE

The Great Council serves as the Expanse's basic legislative body,and has many executive and judicial responsibilities as well.Thus it not only passes the laws of the sector,but also sits in judgment over certain court cases,and confers offices and grants pardons.However,with over 2,000 members,it is far too large to effectively attend to all of its duties. In reality,it mostly exists to elect officials and review the acts of the many smaller councils and committees under it.

You'll notice a pair of numbers by each house.The first number represents the number of votes each house has in the Great Council, and the second number represents the number of worlds in each province.

The votes number is based on total popula tion in each province,not number of worlds held. You can use this to calculate how coalitions change when new alliances are made.The current Mecetti-Reena-Calipsa coalition has the majority with six votes.Any coalition amassing morethan six votes will surpass it,unless Mecetti gains another ally as well.

In handling crucial issues of foreign policy, finance,and internal security,the prime minister may bypass the Senate by taking proposals to the Council of Ten, an inner circle of senior Great Council members (which actually has 35 members).Time in office is limited to two years,after which the officeholder must take a two-year break before becoming eligible to hold the same office again.However, it is not unusual to see a departing member return in a different capacity just a few months later.This system was established to avoid prolonged concentration of power in single individuals while keeping the Council of Ten in experienced hands.Occasionally,it even works.

The Great Council has become very exclusive over time,and doesnot admit new families when old ones die out, as it did in the Dynastic Era. Representatives of the Freeworlds and pettyknightsareadmittedintothe Great Council only very rarely, and are never fully accepted or trusted with positions of authority.

## The Court System

Thoughthenoblesaredefinitelyastepabove the commoners in terms of right to rule,both classes are equal in the eyes of the law.Because the common citizen knows he lives in a just society,he is less likely to feel slighted or discriminated against when the law rules against him in favor of a noble.

![Image](../images/image_000001_89608f3ebd0e0efcc711ba60c566ed15883a4fbb99821f3f389bfbcc58c9b7de.png)

Naturally,the system isnotentirelyequal.A commoner's word may carry as much weight as a noble's before the magistrates and judges of the bench, but nobles can usually afford better lawyers.Too,the principle of equal justice works better in theory than in application, since judges are themselves appointed to the bench by the nobles of the Great Council.However, the perception of impartiality is firmly entrenched in the social fabric of the sector, and obvious abuses are rare.

The Tapani courts are beginning to feel the stress of the external Imperialsystem,as more openlypro-Imperialjudgesascendtothebench. Some court observers have noticed a disturbing trend of Imperial favoritism in such court rooms. Aliens especially have discovered a waning interest in upholding their rights.

Thus far,however,this pro-Imperial trend has yet to break out openly and undermine the entire judicial system.It is only a matter of time,however,before the Tapani courts go the way of courts elsewhere in the Empire; justice for the loyal accepted, none for the outcast.
