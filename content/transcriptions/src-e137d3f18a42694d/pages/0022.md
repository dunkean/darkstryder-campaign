## Adventure Hook: Chained Cargo

The traders are approached by  a  blackmarketeer who wishes to hire them to transport a load of  unspecified 'cargo'  to a specified planet, for trip/e the standard  rate.  He states that he is paying the traders a higher rate  because  he  wishes  to  avoid  'Imperial entanglements.'  Fairly persistent questioning reveals  that  the  cargo  is  sentient  beings: Twi'leks, Wookiees. or some other unfortunate  species  which  is  often  enslaved.  The slave trade is  not illegal in the Empire; however, the planet to which the slaves are being transported has banned the practice and it i s illegal to import them there.

Thereareanumberof places theadventure can go from here:

- The traders may simply refuse  the cargo, in which case the person who tried to hire them may at first increase his offer, or, if they continue to refuse,  threaten them in some way. The slave-trader may cause the traders no little trouble if  and when they return to this planet. and they may find that they have no choice but to destroy the trader for  their own survival.
- The traders  may  be so outraged  by  the thought o f   the  slave-trade  that they decideon their own to go up against the trader. Chaos ensues.
- The traders may  pretend  to agree to the deal, and then, once they have the 'cargo'  on board, kill  the slave-trader's  guards and free the  slaves,  perhaps claimingattackbypirates. The slave-trader probably won't buy it, and

will  send bounty  hunters after the traders. The player characters  can  attempt to smooth things over with the slavers, take the slavers and the bounty hunters out once and for  all, or simply  run away and hope that the slavers forget  about them.

- The traders' greed  may overwhelm their decency and they may choose to accept the cargo. Once aboard ship,  the slaves make an attempt at freedom and the traders have to kill  many of  them to keep them down. When theyreach theother planet,  local lawenforcement officials have heard of the  shipment  and are waiting  for them  at their  drop-point. A fiercebattleensues,and  thetradersareforced to flee  without getting paid.

Note: Carrying  slaves as cargo is contemptible. Han Solo wouldn't do it, no matter how desperate  he was for money, and your players shouldn't either. If they do,  punish them for it. Send  the  forces o f justiceafterthem.  Havethem chased by friends  and family of the enslaved beings, who have sworn blood-feud against all who engage in the slave trade. The Alliance is vehemently against  slavery: if  they have any ContactswiththeRebeIs,  thetraderslose them. Other tramp captains will refuse to associate with them. In short, the player characters become outcasts and outlaws. The only way for them to clear their names  i s   to destroy the slavetrader who got them into this. At  heart, Star Wars is  a  game about good versus evil: dealing in slavery i s   every bit  as terrible as turning to the dark side o f   the Force.
